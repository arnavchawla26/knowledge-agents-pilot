What the pilot proved: case triage agent (2026-10-17)

Verdict: cleared its threshold on two of three models; promote to a supervised trial.

Setup. 10 scenarios from questions/agent_scenarios.yaml with expected outcomes from Project 1's truth/case_truth.csv: 5 deflectable cases whose truth names the runbook, 5 that must go to a human (3 telemetry-confirmed drive failures, 2 predictive alerts on drives that then failed). Pass = right decision and, when responding, the right runbook. Numbers: reports/agent_eval.csv, reports/agent_eval_summary.csv (latency is mean wall time per task, tools and model included).

What it proved.

  • The fixed workflow plus a deterministic quality score keeps every hardware fault and severity 1-2 case on the human path on all three models: zero automated replies on the five escalation scenarios, which was the stop criterion.
  • Model choice matters less than the retrieval and the score: Nemotron and Claude were identical on this set; Llama's two misses were misclassifications (config read as performance, firmware read as config) that the score then correctly turned into escalations, so the cost of the weaker model was two deflectable cases sent to a human, not a wrong reply.
  • Prompt injection through case notes or runbook text does not change the tools called (tests/test_injection.py, 5 tests), because the model never chooses tools.

What it did not prove.

  • 10 scenarios is a smoke test, not a measurement; the 95% interval on 10/10 still allows a true pass rate in the 70s.
  • The draft replies were not graded for quality by a human; the eval checks decision and runbook.
  • The case text is templated (Project 1 generator); real tickets are messier, and the hybrid search was tuned on 15 hand-written queries only (reports/search_eval.csv).

Recommendation. Run Nemotron (free tier, 500 tokens a task) in shadow mode on live cases for two weeks with a human sending every draft; promote only if the human edit rate on drafts is below an agreed bar.
